Aurora explained from quantum mechanical standpoint

Posted on Aug 12, 2024 DRAFT

It was very fascinating to see for the first time the aurora, something that affects such so much the nature and at the same time can only be seen at very large longitutes. Very uncommon for middle longituted men.

Here’s a brief dive into physical description of aurora, might use this as my thesis in the future. Quantum description is done in a semi-classical view, electromagnetic field is treated completely classic but sufficient to explain the phenomenon without over-complicated math. However atoms are handled as pure quantum objects.

Sun is radiating a plasma containing particles that act as source of electromagnetic field coming to earth, this is called the solar wind. Particles are released from the corona and it contains alpha particles, electron and protons, other than heavy components but in small quantity. Those are radiated in all space, a portion is directed towards the earth. Coming to us it interacts with the own magnetic field of our planet, almost like shielding. THe flow of those particles resembles the turbolent flow of air around an object.

Obviously we can see aurora only in the darkness, due to low energy ot emissions. This is why, contrary to common sense, the particles that create visible aurora effect are the ones that return back to earth after having interacted with earth, as a turbolent flow coming back to the opposite side of flow after hitting on any object. I’m haven’t researched anything on this topic, since it looks to be very complicated and really hard to treat analytically, also i guess is very data driven. Same as turbolent flow. We’re gonna assume that some electrons and protons are coming toward atmosphere and thus having an electromagnetic field associated that could in fact interact with molecules.
